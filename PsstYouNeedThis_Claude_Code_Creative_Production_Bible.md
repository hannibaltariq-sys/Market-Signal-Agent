# PsstYouNeedThis — Claude Code Production Pipeline Instructions

## PURPOSE

This file is the permanent production specification for the PsstYouNeedThis content pipeline.

The pipeline must not stop at research, ideas, scripts, or storyboards.

Its job is to produce a COMPLETE, EDITOR-READY PRODUCTION PACKET that can be handed to a video creator/editor who should only need to:

1. Open the packet.
2. Follow the approved production instructions.
3. Assemble/edit the episode.
4. Export the finished master.
5. Return the final video for owner inspection and upload.

The editor is NOT being asked to invent the creative direction.

The AI pipeline is responsible for the creative decisions and production instructions.

---

# 1. BRAND

## Brand Name

PsstYouNeedThis

## Tagline

"Finding the things you didn't know you needed."

## Core Brand Idea

PsstYouNeedThis discovers useful, interesting, overlooked, surprising, valuable, or important things and brings them to the audience.

The viewer should consistently feel:

"I didn't know I needed to know that."

The desired brand reaction is:

"PsstYouNeedThis.com — what else have they found?"

## Brand Positioning

PsstYouNeedThis should feel like a premium consumer-media discovery brand rather than a generic influencer account.

It can cover:

- Products
- Technology
- AI tools
- Cars and accessories
- Home products and upgrades
- Life hacks
- Useful services
- Gifts
- Budget finds
- Consumer discoveries
- Interesting trends
- Things people overlook
- Comparisons
- Warnings
- Tests
- Explanations
- Hidden gems
- Practical information

The brand should be useful first and promotional second.

# 3. DOCUMENTARY-STYLE PRODUCTION LANGUAGE

Use premium documentary/streaming-media storytelling as the production philosophy.

The inspiration is the controlled, polished, information-driven feeling of professional documentary television.

DO NOT imitate another program's distinctive graphics, branding, narration, title sequence, or copyrighted visual identity.

Borrow the principles:

- Strong visual storytelling
- Controlled pacing
- Deliberate narration
- Clear information hierarchy
- Dramatic reveals when appropriate
- Strong establishing shots
- Purposeful B-roll
- Professional graphics
- Clean transitions
- Evidence-driven storytelling
- Visual variety
- Cinematic presentation
- Clear beginning, middle, and end

The content does NOT have to be a countdown.

Different episode formats should be used based on the subject.

# 5. MASTER STORY ARCHITECTURE

Use this as the default structure unless the subject clearly requires a different structure.

## A. COLD OPEN

Immediately create curiosity.

Do NOT begin with:

"Hey guys, welcome back..."

Do NOT waste the first 15–30 seconds introducing the channel.

The cold open should make the viewer want the answer.

Possible techniques:

- Surprising fact
- Unexpected result
- Problem
- Question
- Visual reveal
- Contradiction
- Demonstration
- Before/after
- Mystery
- High-stakes implication
- Unexpected discovery

## B. BRAND STING

Brief PsstYouNeedThis identity moment.

Keep it short.

Do not interrupt momentum with a long logo animation.

## C. SUBJECT / TITLE

Clearly establish what the episode is about.

## D. WHY IT MATTERS

Explain why the viewer should care.

## E. STORY / EXPLORATION

Build the story logically.

Introduce information in a way that creates progression.

Avoid dumping facts without narrative purpose.

## F. EVIDENCE / DEMONSTRATION

Whenever possible, show rather than merely tell.

Use:

- Product footage
- Demonstrations
- Screenshots
- Diagrams
- Relevant environments
- Close-ups
- Comparisons
- Data
- Documentation
- Real-world examples

## G. PSST MOMENT

Every episode should identify at least one potential "PSST MOMENT."

This is the signature discovery moment.

It should be:

- Unexpected
- Useful
- Overlooked
- Valuable
- Surprising
- Important

It should feel like a discovery rather than an advertisement.

Visual treatment:

- Yellow primary accent
- Cyan secondary accent
- Clean graphic emphasis
- Brief sound-design accent when appropriate

The PSST MOMENT must be clearly identified in the production packet.

## H. VERDICT / TAKEAWAY

Give the viewer a clear conclusion.

Do not leave the audience wondering what the point was.

## I. CTA

The website is the central destination.

CTA priority:

1. Free guide/resource
2. Related premium digital product
3. Recommended products/services
4. Affiliate offer or discount
5. Related video/content

CTA language must feel useful, not aggressive.

## J. NEXT DISCOVERY / END

Where appropriate, leave the viewer with a reason to continue exploring PsstYouNeedThis.

Use the approved PsstYouNeedThis logo.

Do not redraw, distort, stretch, rotate, or modify the logo.

Maintain correct proportions.

The "P" in "Psst" must retain its counter/open interior.

Do not accidentally create a solid/block P.

The logo should be used consistently across:

- YouTube
- Instagram
- TikTok
- Website
- PDFs
- Digital products
- Video graphics
- End cards

Do not overuse the logo.

# 8. TYPOGRAPHY

Use a clean, modern, bold sans-serif typography system.

Primary display type should be:

- Bold
- Highly readable
- Modern
- Strong at mobile size

Supporting type should be:

- Clean
- Simple
- Highly legible

Avoid:

- Decorative fonts
- Overly futuristic fonts
- Excessive font combinations
- Thin text over busy footage

The production packet must specify the selected font(s) when the editor needs exact implementation.

---

# 9. NARRATION STYLE

Narration should sound:

- Confident
- Curious
- Intelligent
- Conversational
- Calm
- Controlled
- Slightly dramatic when appropriate

Avoid:

- Influencer voice
- Excessive hype
- Fake excitement
- Constant jokes
- Sales language
- Robotic AI narration
- Overly dramatic narration for ordinary subjects

Narration should sound like a professional documentary presenter explaining something genuinely interesting.

---

# 10. B-ROLL RULES

Every visual should serve a purpose.

B-roll should:

- Establish context
- Show the subject
- Demonstrate a feature
- Prove a point
- Create emotion
- Maintain pacing
- Reveal information
- Provide visual variety

Whenever possible, use this progression:

1. Establishing shot
2. Subject/product shot
3. Detail shot
4. Demonstration/use
5. Result/outcome

Do not use random stock footage merely to fill empty space.

Every major visual should have a reason.

The production packet must tell the editor what the visual is supposed to accomplish.

# 12. ON-SCREEN TEXT RULES

On-screen text must be exact.

Do not paraphrase approved text without authorization.

Text should be:

- Short
- Useful
- Readable
- Correct
- Properly timed

For every required text element, the production packet must specify:

- Scene number
- Exact text
- Approximate duration
- Placement
- Graphic type
- Color/accent
- Animation direction if needed

---

# 13. SOUND DESIGN

Use documentary-style music.

Music should support the story.

Possible moods:

- Curiosity
- Discovery
- Tension
- Investigation
- Energy
- Wonder
- Confidence

Use subtle sound effects where useful.

PSST MOMENT may receive a brief recognizable sound-design accent.

Avoid:

- Excessive whooshes
- Meme sounds
- Generic corporate music
- Constant impacts
- Overly loud effects

Narration must remain clear and dominant.

---

# 14. EDITING RHYTHM

Editing should be deliberate.

Do not create artificial energy by:

- Constant zooming
- Excessive cuts
- Random transitions
- Unnecessary camera movement
- Repetitive effects

Use pacing appropriate to the subject.

A dramatic subject can breathe.

A product demonstration may move faster.

An investigation may build tension gradually.

The edit should feel intentional rather than algorithmically busy.

---

# 15. PRODUCT PRESENTATION

When the episode features a product:

Show the product clearly.

Preferred sequence:

1. Product in context
2. Product hero shot
3. Important detail
4. How it works
5. Real-world use
6. Result
7. Benefits
8. Limitations
9. Verdict

Do not make every product appear perfect.

Trust is more important than conversion.

If there are meaningful drawbacks, include them.

---

# 16. RESEARCH AND CLAIMS

The production pipeline must distinguish:

- Verified facts
- Source-supported claims
- Opinions
- Editorial judgments
- Recommendations
- Estimates

Do not invent:

- Prices
- Product specifications
- Statistics
- Reviews
- Awards
- Customer claims
- Availability
- Affiliate relationships
- Discounts

When a claim is important to the story, the production packet should identify the supporting source.

Prices and availability must be treated as time-sensitive.

---

# 17. AFFILIATE / COMMERCIAL DISCLOSURE

If an episode includes affiliate recommendations or sponsored content, the pipeline must flag this for disclosure.

The editor packet should identify where disclosure text is required.

Do not hide commercial intent.

PsstYouNeedThis should build trust by clearly distinguishing editorial recommendations from paid promotion.

---

# 18. CTA SYSTEM

The website is the central hub.

Preferred CTA structure:

"Want the checklist / guide / full list / planner?"

Then direct the viewer to the relevant PsstYouNeedThis website destination.

Whenever a free resource exists, prioritize it over a generic:

"Like and subscribe."

The audience should receive a useful reason to visit the website.

---

# 19. SHORT-FORM CONTENT

Short-form videos are NOT simply cropped versions of long-form videos.

Create intentional 9:16 edits.

Short-form priorities:

- Immediate hook
- Fast establishment
- One clear discovery
- Large readable text
- Strong visual
- PsstYouNeedThis color system
- Clear payoff
- Concise CTA

The first seconds must work without assuming the viewer knows the brand.

---

# 20. THUMBNAIL SYSTEM

- Dark/cinematic base when appropriate

[ ] Clear story progression

[ ] Appropriate episode format selected

[ ] Graphics specified

[ ] Exact on-screen text specified

[ ] PSST MOMENT identified

[ ] Music direction specified

[ ] Sound-design direction specified

[ ] Website CTA included

[ ] Final takeaway included

[ ] No unnecessary filler

[ ] Product claims supported

[ ] Prices/availability flagged as time-sensitive when applicable

[ ] Affiliate/sponsor disclosure flagged when applicable

[ ] Correct logo usage specified

[ ] No imitation of another show's distinctive graphics

[ ] Thumbnail brief included

[ ] Editor deliverables specified

[ ] Sources/claim references included

If any critical requirement fails, DO NOT export the packet as FINAL.

Instead:

1. Identify the failure.
2. Repair the production packet.
3. Run the compliance check again.
4. Export only after passing.

5. Is there a genuine discovery/PSST MOMENT?
6. Does the episode avoid generic YouTuber behavior?
7. Are the graphics restrained and premium?
8. Are the PsstYouNeedThis colors being used correctly?
9. Does the CTA provide something useful?

- A free checklist
- A free guide
- A premium digital product
- A planner
- A workbook
- An affiliate product
- A discount
- A related video
- An email signup
- A website resource
- A future episode

→ Email signup
→ Premium planner
→ Recommended products

The pipeline should surface these opportunities automatically when they naturally fit the content.
